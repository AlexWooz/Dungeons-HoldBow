# Technical notes

What we've learned about keeping the bow in hand in Minecraft Dungeons (v1.17.0.0, UE 4.22, Steam). The general modding constraints (Blueprint only, Blueprint Loader levels, no UE4SS, no `PrintString`) are the same as ThirdPersonCam's; see its `docs/TECHNICAL.md`.

**Goal:** after a shot, the bow stays in hand, including while walking with the default (melee) animations, until a melee attack. The melee weapon then stays out until the next shot.

**Status:** probe v12.1 **works**, tested in camp on controller: after a shot the bow stays in hand while walking (default melee run animation) until a melee press. The fix is the game's own call, `PlayerCharacter.EquipmentDisplayComponent.ShowWeaponSlot(RangedWeapon)`, made every frame while holding (see "The real mechanism"). Everything before v12 is a record of what didn't work. Still a probe: long readout, B/N keys, A / Left Mouse release.

## How the game shows weapons (findings)
- **The hero's weapon slots are empty.** `BP_AlexCharacter` has two `DungeonsChildActorComponent`s tagged `MeleeWeapon` / `RangedWeapon` on `CharacterMesh0` at `J_R_Weapon_Socket` / `J_L_Weapon_Socket`. `ChildActor` is None and nothing is attached under them, so their visibility does nothing (probe v1).
- **Weapons are separate actors** in the hero's `GetAttachedActors`: e.g. `BP_Bow_C_3`, `BP_Hammer_Unique1_C_3` (plus the armor, `BP_MercenaryArmor_Unique1_C_3`). Their root component isn't attached (parent None). Their meshes are attached straight to the hero mesh instead:
  - bow: `SK_BowRigTpose` (a skeletal mesh) on `CharacterMesh0@J_L_Weapon_Socket`, with `StaticMesh` and `SM_Bow2..4` on its `J_Bow1Socket..J_Bow4Socket` bones;
  - hammer: `SM_Hammer` on `CharacterMesh0@J_R_Weapon_Socket`.
  So the reliable way to find them is: any scene component of an attached actor that sits on `J_L_Weapon_Socket` (ranged) or `J_R_Weapon_Socket` (melee) (probe v3.3).
- **Swapping uses actor `bHidden`.** Idle: bow hidden, melee shown. While shooting: bow shown, melee hidden. This is triggered by anim notify states in the attack animations (`AnimNS_Player_ShowWeaponSlot`, `AnimNS_Player_HideWeapon`), which cast the owner to `BP_AlexCharacter` (or the menu's `BP_3DPlayerCharacterRepresentation`).
- **Montages:** shots play `Player_Bow_Charge_Montage`; a melee attack (A) played `AnimMontage_1`. Rolls and other actions haven't been checked yet. The montage name is a better "melee started" signal than button presses: Left Mouse is also click-to-move on keyboard and mouse, so ending the hold on Left Mouse put the bow away whenever you walked.
- **Hiding the melee weapon works:** `SetActorHiddenInGame(true)` every frame (actor ticks in `TG_PostUpdateWork`, set in BeginPlay) keeps it hidden. The hero still uses the melee idle/run pose.
- **Visibility flags aren't enough to show the bow.** While holding, the bow actor is `bHidden = false`, `SK_BowRigTpose` is visible (with `SetVisibility(true, propagate)`), `bHiddenInGame = false` (with `SetHiddenInGame(false, propagate)`), and its world scale is 1. The bow is still not drawn (probe v4.1).
- **The bow is a set of alternative meshes picked by bone scale** (probe v5). `SK_BowRigTpose` runs an `AnimSingleNodeInstance` (one animation asset, driven by the bow notifies in `Player_Bow_Charge_Montage`: `BP_BowLoadNotifyState`, `AnimNS_BowLoad`, `BP_BowChargeNotifyState`, `AnimNS_BowFire`, ...). It scales the bones so only one part is full size: at rest `StaticMesh` is 1.0 and `SM_Bow2..4` are 0.01; while aiming `SM_Bow4` is 1.0 and `StaticMesh` 0.01. The bow you see in hand while aiming is `SM_Bow4`. `StaticMesh` at 1.0 draws nothing visible in hand.

## The real mechanism (FModel bytecode export)
`AnimNS_Player_ShowWeaponSlot` (used in the attack animations) doesn't touch visibility flags. It casts the mesh owner to `BP_AlexCharacter` (or the menu's `BP_3DPlayerCharacterRepresentation`) and calls the native component **`PlayerCharacter.EquipmentDisplayComponent`** (`PlayerView.EquipmentDisplayComponent` for the menu actor):
- `Received_NotifyBegin` → `EquipmentDisplayComponent.ShowWeaponSlot(SlotType)`, with `SlotType` an `ESlotType` (`Armor`, `MeleeWeapon`, `RangedWeapon`, ...; the numeric order is unknown);
- `Received_NotifyEnd` → `EquipmentDisplayComponent.RestoreWeaponVisibility()`.

Whatever actually makes the bow render happens in that native code, which is why copying every observable flag, transform, animation and material never showed it. Other assets in the export: `Actors/Equipment/RangedWeapons/Bow/BP_Bow` (+ `SM_Bow1..4`, `MI_Bow`), the bow rig animations `AllBowAssets/Animations/BowRig_{Idle,Load,Loaded,Fire,...}`, and the bow notifies (`AllBowAssets/AnimNS_Bow{Load,Loaded,Fire}`, `Notifies/Bow/BP_Bow*NotifyState`).

## Probe v12 (current build)
- New Mod Kit stub `Source/Dungeons/PlayerCharacter.h` (editor-only, like ThirdPersonCam's `BasePlayerController.h`): `APlayerCharacter : ACharacter` with `UEquipmentDisplayComponent* EquipmentDisplayComponent`, `UEquipmentDisplayComponent` with `ShowWeaponSlot(ESlotType)` and `RestoreWeaponVisibility()`, and `ESlotType` with placeholder values `Slot0..Slot7`. Blueprint stores enum literals as numbers, so the probe finds the ranged value at runtime: "Enum to String" uses the game's real enum, and the value whose name contains `Ranged` is picked. The readout lists the real names for 0–7.
- While holding outside `Player_Bow_Charge_Montage`, every frame: `Cast To PlayerCharacter` → `EquipmentDisplayComponent.ShowWeaponSlot(ranged)`. On a melee press (A / Left Mouse) the hold ends and the probe also calls `RestoreWeaponVisibility()`.
- N cycles 0 off, 1 `ShowWeaponSlot` (start value), 2 + the v10 pose pin, 3 + the v11 `J_L_Hand` re-attach.

### v12 result → v12.1 (current build)
`ESlotType` values 0–7 are item/ammo types: `HealthPotion`, `Arrow`, `BurningArrow`, `FireworksArrow`, `TormentProjectile`, `HeavyHarpoon`, `ThunderingArrow`, `VoidArrow`. `RangedWeapon` comes later, so v12 never found it and never called `ShowWeaponSlot`. v12.1 scans one value per frame at the start of Tick (0..255) until "Enum to String" gives exactly `RangedWeapon`, then keeps it (readout: `found`, `value`, `scan`).

**Result: `RangedWeapon` = 15**, found within a second of loading. With `ShowWeaponSlot(15)` every frame while holding, **the bow stays visible in hand while walking** until the melee press. No crash on quit.

**Other weapons (v12.1):** `BP_Longbow` and `BP_ExplodingCrossbow` were found on the same socket and stayed in hand while walking. During melee swings only the melee weapon shows, and the melee weapon stays out after a swing as in the normal game.

**Release build:** `DEBUG = False` in `gen.py` skips creating `W_HoldBowDebug` and leaves the B / N / M key events unconnected. M (put the hero's material on the bow) would otherwise re-skin the bow until a restart. Everything else is the v12.1 probe: mode 1 (`ShowWeaponSlot` while holding) is the start value, so the v4–v11 experiments are built but inactive.

## Probe v11 results
- `J_L_Weapon_Socket` sits exactly on `J_L_Hand` (distance 0), and `SM_Bow4` is about 4 units from the hand while holding. **The bow is in the hand, full size, visible, with the aiming animation asset and position, and still not drawn.** Everything Blueprint can observe matches the aiming state, so the game hides it in a way we can't see from outside.
- N = 2 (re-attach to `J_L_Hand`) was a regression: the bow is then no longer on `J_L_Weapon_Socket`, so detection loses it (`Bow None`) and the hammer comes back. N = 1 is the start value again.
- Next step: read the game's own logic. Export with FModel, with Kismet bytecode: `BP_Bow`, `AnimNS_Player_ShowWeaponSlot`, `AnimNS_Player_HideWeapon`, and the bow notify states (`BP_BowLoadNotifyState`, `BP_BowChargeNotifyState`, `BP_BowFireNotifyState`, `AnimNS_BowLoad`, `AnimNS_BowFire`).

## Probe v11
v10 results: **no crash on quit.** While holding, the aiming asset was restored and the position pinned (0.083), so the bow was in the same state it's in while aiming. Still nothing was drawn.

The last untested difference is *where* the bow is: it hangs from the hero's `J_L_Weapon_Socket`, and the hero's non-bow animations may move that socket out of view. v11:
- readout: distance from `J_L_Weapon_Socket` to the hero's `J_L_Hand`, and from `SM_Bow4` to `J_L_Hand`;
- **N = 2** (the new start value): pin the pose as in v10, **and** re-attach the bow skeleton to `CharacterMesh0@J_L_Hand` (`K2_AttachToComponent`, snap location and rotation, keep world scale). It goes back to `J_L_Weapon_Socket` as soon as pinning stops: the next shot, the end of the hold, or N off. N = 1 is v10, N = 0 is off.

## Probe v10
v9 result: pinning at maxPos only reached **0.405**, the length of the animation the bow plays *after* the shot. During the shot it read 0.5, so the bow plays **different animation assets** (load/charge, then fire/put away). Pinning the time of the put-away asset can't reproduce the aiming pose. Still no bow while holding. **The game crashed on quit** with v9 (cause unknown; v9 touched the bow every frame).

v10:
- **Record** while the hero is in `Player_Bow_Charge_Montage` **and `SM_Bow4` is full size** (the state in which you see the bow): the bow animation's asset (`AnimSingleNodeInstance.GetAnimationAsset`, readout `aimAsset`) and position (`aimPos`).
- **While holding** outside the montage, every frame: if the bow plays a different asset, `SetAnimationAsset(aimAsset, looping false, rate 1)`, then `SetPosition(aimPos, no notifies)`. **N** toggles this (on by default).
- **EndPlay** sets `Ending`, and the tick then stops touching the weapons, in case the quit crash came from touching them during teardown.

## Probe v9
v8 results:
- `SM_Bow4`'s material 0 is a runtime `MaterialInstanceDynamic`. None of the guessed fade parameter names exist (all read 0). The bow skeleton has no material at all (it's only a rig).
- **M confirmed `SM_Bow4` is the bow seen while aiming**: after M, the aiming bow was drawn in the hero's skin material. But it stayed invisible while holding even with that plain material, so **the material isn't what hides it**.
- While aiming, the bow animation sits at position **0.5**; it ends the shot at about 0.115. v7 pinned 0 or 0.115, where the parts read the same scales but nothing showed. They're probably posed somewhere out of view.

v9 records the highest bow-animation position seen during the shot (`maxPos`) and adds **N = 3: pin at maxPos** (the aiming pose), now the start value. N cycles 0 off, 1 lastPos, 2 position 0, 3 maxPos.

## Probe v8
v7 with N=2 (position 0) put the bow parts into exactly the aiming configuration while holding (`SM_Bow4` 1.0, `StaticMesh` 0.01, actor shown, meshes visible), yet still nothing was drawn. So the difference between aiming and holding isn't transform or visibility. v8 tests the material:
- readout: the name of `SM_Bow4`'s material 0 and the bow skeleton's material 0, and, if `SM_Bow4`'s is a `MaterialInstanceDynamic`, its values for `Opacity`, `Dissolve`, `DissolveAmount`, `Fade`, `Alpha`, `Visibility`, `Appear`, `Erode`, `Glow` and `Transition` (0 when the parameter doesn't exist);
- **M** (one-way until restart): put the hero mesh's material 0 on `SM_Bow4` and on the bow skeleton. If a skin-textured bow then appears while holding, the original material is what hides it;
- N now starts at 2 (position 0, the aiming configuration).

## Probe v7
v6 forced `SM_Bow2` to world scale 1 while holding. The readout confirmed it, but still no bow was drawn. So the visible bow is most likely `SK_BowRigTpose`'s own skinned geometry, collapsed by its animation (it reads `vis=false` at rest). Blueprint can't set bone scales, but the bow plays a single animation, so v7 controls its time instead:
- while the hero is in `Player_Bow_Charge_Montage`, record the bow animation's `GetPosition()` every frame (`LastPos`);
- while holding outside that montage, `SetPosition(pos, bFireNotifies = false)` every frame. **N** cycles: 0 off, 1 `LastPos` (start value), 2 position 0.
- The v6 part scaling is gone. `SetWorldScale3D` changes the part's relative scale, so it could leave parts oversized on the next shot (until a restart).
- The readout adds the bow animation's `pos` and `lastPos`.

## Probe v6
Like v5 below, except **N** cycled which bow part was forced to full size (`SetWorldScale3D(1,1,1)` every frame, after the game's animation) while holding and outside `Player_Bow_Charge_Montage`: 0 off, 1 `SM_Bow2`, 2 `SM_Bow3`, 3 `SM_Bow4`. Result: `SM_Bow2` read scale 1.0, but no bow was visible.

## Probe v5
- Finds the bow and melee actors (and the bow's `SK_BowRigTpose`) by hand socket every frame.
- **Holding** starts when the game shows the bow (a shot). While holding, every frame: bow actor shown, melee actor hidden, bow mesh `SetVisibility(true)` + `SetHiddenInGame(false)` (both propagated).
- **A / Left Mouse** end the hold and restore the game's state (bow hidden, melee shown).
- **B** turns the feature off. **N** toggles an experiment: while holding and not in `Player_Bow_Charge_Montage`, stop `SK_BowRigTpose` from ticking, so its animation can't collapse the bow.
- **Readout:** holding state, both weapons' hidden flags, bow mesh visible / hiddenInGame / scale, the hero's montage and a log of all montages seen, the bow skeleton's anim class and montage, and every bow component's world scale and visibility.
